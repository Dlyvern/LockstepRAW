# Story bible

Status: draft. Everything marked **OPEN** waits for a decision from the user.
The **SPOILERS** section at the end is for the team only; nothing in it is ever stated in
the first missions.

## Premise

Two broke cops share a shabby flat in a rundown American district. They work the
worst-funded precinct in a fictional city, and on top of patrol they are on the precinct's
part-time response team. Level 1 starts as a grounded cop story: a morning, a shift, then a
hostage call at a city hospital. The horror creeps in under the hospital and never announces
itself before that.

## The leads (Player 1 and Player 2)

Flatmates, partners, same rank, same pay (not enough). Lines address them together ("you
two") or work for either one; neither is "the main one".

**Their job (proposed, OPEN for the user):** patrol officers by day, and volunteers on the
14th Precinct's **Special Response Team (SRT)**: six officers who get called up when the city
team is busy. They get a small standby stipend, hand-me-down gear, and the new V-45s. Small
real departments run part-time teams like this, so the broke-flatmates life stays as it is
and the hospital raid still makes sense. Alternative: make them full-time tactical officers
(then the station lines about patrol and the "only car that starts" need a pass).

- **Player 1** - the one who keeps the flat (barely) running: pays the bills late, fixes the
  boiler with tape. Patient, dry, notices things.
  Name options (**OPEN**): Dan Harlow / Eddie Marsh / Tom Kessel
- **Player 2** - the one who's always one bad week from quitting. Quick mouth, good shot, bad
  with money. Loyal past the point of sense.
  Name options (**OPEN**): Nick Varga / Joe Lindqvist / Ray Okafor

Both are men in the current draft because the chief's lines say "gentlemen". **OPEN:** confirm,
or say if either lead should be a woman (the lines change).

Addressing rule: in level 1 NPCs may say "you two". **After level 1, NPCs only ever address
one person** (see SPOILERS). Write later levels with that in mind.

## The chief

Chief of the precinct, 50s, office on the 2nd floor that's too big for the furniture in it.
Sarcastic about money because he's the one who fights for it. On the surface he cares about
his people and would rather die than say so. Placeholder name: **Chief Roy Calder** (**OPEN**).
During the hospital he is the voice on the radio, always pushing them down.

## The station (14th Precinct)

Lived-in and worth caring about: after level 1 the chief tells the leads their squad is dead,
so the people here must be specific now.

- **Desk sergeant** (lobby) - 31 years at the desk, 29 days to retirement, dreams of a lake.
- **Armorer** (basement cage) - counts every round, proud of a daughter on the honor roll
  (photo taped to the cage).
- **Range officer** (6-lane range) - 20 years on the range, half deaf, gruff.
- **Holding guard** (basement cells) - buys prisoners sandwiches with his own money.
- **Bullpen** - tired officers, frozen overtime, a broken copier, Rodriguez bringing cake Friday.
- **Prisoners** - drunks, petty thieves, one man who keeps asking the time.

**OPEN:** names for the sergeant, armorer, range officer and guard (left unnamed in dialogue
for now so they can be named once).

## The squad (the rest of the SRT)

Four officers who go into the hospital with the leads. They are on the radio all level and
fight their way down after the leads in beat 5 (see SPOILERS), so they need to be people.

- **Squad lead** - a sergeant, steady, by the book, calls the plan at the cordon. Name OPEN.
- **Rodriguez** - the officer bringing cake Friday (bullpen line). Proposal: she's on the team,
  so the cake is never eaten. **OPEN.**
- Two more officers, names OPEN. On the radio as "squad".

## The hospital

**Saint Abel Memorial Hospital** (fictional), a mid-size city hospital in the district: ER and
lobby on the ground floor, three ward floors, a basement with services and the morgue, an
ambulance bay and a parking lot outside. Under it, behind a door marked as the sealed **East
Annex** ("old research wing, closed, asbestos"), is a wing that isn't on the city's plans.

## Weapons

Fictional designations only (no real makers or model names).

- **V-45** - the station's new .45 SMG, issued at the armory. Always "V-45" (plural "V-45s");
  the armorer may add "forty-five calibre". Never "Vector" or a maker's name.

## The district and city

- City: **Port Merrow** (fictional), an old river-port city that lost its industry.
- District: **Carver Street / Saint Abel** - brick tenements, a park nobody maintains,
  boarded shops, one good diner, the hospital. The flat and the station are both here.

**OPEN:** city and district names are placeholders.

## Tone rules

- Grounded, dry, tired. Realistic like Trepang2's world, not comic.
- Humor comes from money, bureaucracy and fatigue, never from winking at the player.
- Nobody explains the plot. People talk about their day.
- Horror is absent in the first missions. At most one or two deniable hints per location,
  each logged in `foreshadowing.md`.
- No real people, police departments, brands or places.
- Short lines: spoken under ~20 words, objectives under ~8, tips one line.

## SPOILERS (team only - never stated in level 1)

- **The program.** A research program runs a hidden lab under Saint Abel Memorial (the
  "East Annex"). Its subject is not a drug or a device: **the thing being tested is the two
  officers themselves.** No glowing MacGuffin.
- **The "terrorists"** are the program's own people (professional contractors) running a
  field trial. They don't hold floors; they move material up and out from below. The
  hostages in the basement are bait.
- **The chief is in on it.** He brings the call to his own team, says the hostages are in the
  basement, and on the radio herds the leads down the whole level. The leads were chosen
  because they are close.
- **The lab.** The leads find it, then gas fills it and both pass out. **OPEN:** did the chief
  trigger the gas remotely, or did the firefight?
- **The chair.** They wake restrained side by side in the same lab. A power cut frees them.
  "Monsters" break in; bullet-time triggers for the first time. They shred them. Blackout,
  title card.
- **The monsters are the squad.** The squad fought down to rescue them; the dosed leads saw
  them as monsters and killed them. They shout the leads' names (distorted), never quite
  shoot back, and move to reach, not attack. Their bodies wear SWAT gear if you look on a
  second playthrough.
- **The last one killed is the other player's character.** Revealed only at the very end of
  the game; invisible on a first playthrough.
- **One mind (ending hook).** The two leads share one mind. Bullet-time is that shared power,
  so it is a single shared state between both players, never per player.
- **Bullet-time rules by stage:** none before the chair; automatic and unlimited in the chair
  fight; afterwards a player-controlled meter (shared).
- **After level 1:** the chief interrogates them: the squad is dead and it's their fault.
  From then on NPCs only ever address one person.
